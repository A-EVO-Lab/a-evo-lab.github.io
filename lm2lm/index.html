Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models

LM2LM trains a language model with reinforcement learning to propose improvements to other language models. Training sampled 24,576 proposals but ran only 200 experiments for real; a research world model supplied the rewards. On held-out tasks, the trained Qwen3.8 27B proposes better changes than GPT-6 Sol with 300 real experimental records in context.

Paper (PDF) arXiv:2610.23344 BibTeX

How it works. A frozen LLM serves as a research world model (RWM): given real experimental records in context, it predicts how much each proposal would improve the target model, and those predictions are the RL reward. Between training segments, the proposals it rates highest are built and run for real, and their measured outcomes are added to its records.

Method

RL on rewards from a research world model, recalibrated with targeted real runs

  1. Ground the reward. A frozen GPT-6 Sol, the RWM, reads 100 real experimental records in context and predicts each proposal’s gain over the task’s reference recipe.
  2. Train the proposer. Qwen3.8 27B is trained with DAPO for 96 steps on rewards built from the predicted gains.
  3. Calibrate on-policy. After steps 16, 40, and 64, the proposals the RWM rates highest are built by GPT-6 Astra and run for real (31, 38, and 31 runs). Their measured outcomes join the RWM’s records, so the rewards keep up with what the proposer now generates.

Results

On held-out tasks, LM2LM’s proposals yield 2.77× the average gain of GPT-6 Sol with 300 records in context

Training also removes harmful proposals: the share with negative gain falls from 70.4% to 39.2% on autoregressive tasks and from 59.7% to 7.6% on diffusion tasks.

Cost

Rewards for 24,576 proposals cost $0.9k. Running them all would have cost about $4.8 million.

LM2LM’s total, $40.1k, is under 1% of running every proposal for real and 33% below the in-context baselines.

Transfer

On a target family held out of training, LM2LM’s average gain is 2.48× that of the strongest baseline

In a separate experiment the whole Qwen3 target family is held out: no Qwen3 records in proposer training, RWM grounding, or the baselines’ context. The proposer learns from the other seven families.

Analysis

Without calibration, the world model’s ranking accuracy falls from 0.72 to 0.37, below chance

Training is extended to 128 steps and compared with a static variant whose RWM keeps its initial 100 records. Both are checked against real runs on held-out autoregressive states.

Which runs the budget is spent on matters

Static: the initial 100 records plus 100 random ones, never refreshed. Random: refreshed with random valid proposals. Paper Figure 4.

One overestimated change, corrected

A change to the number of tokens per optimizer update on NanoChat has no record in the initial set, and the RWM overrates it. With static records it grows to 79% of NanoChat training proposals by step 64. LM2LM’s first calibration runs seven such proposals, five of which degrade the reference recipe, and its share falls to 8%.

Case studies in the paper (p. 26).

Limitations

What did not work, and what this does not show

  • Fine-tuning on the 300 past records (SFT, RFT-Real) ends below the untuned base model on autoregressive tasks: −12.19 and −22.95 Avg@4, against −7.94.
  • LM2LM trails GPT-6 Sol with records on two families, Marin and E2D2-SW. On NanoChat, GPT-6 Sol with records is the only method with a positive Avg@4.
  • 39.2% of LM2LM’s autoregressive proposals are still worse than the reference recipe. For GPT-6 Sol with records the figure is 29.6%.

The study covers proposal-level improvements in language-model development, not end-to-end research campaigns. Results depend on the world model, builder, target environments, and experiment budget; they do not establish open-ended self-improvement.

Tasks

40 training tasks and 10 held-out tasks across eight model families

Each task specifies a model family, size, training budget, and reference recipe. Every executed proposal trains its target model from scratch.
